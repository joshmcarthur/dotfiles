## Avoid stacked one-liner sentences

After breaking `and`-chains and adding contractions, rewrites often land on a different tell: a run of short, complete sentences that each drop a new beat. It reads punchy and "human" on skim, but aloud it sounds like copy written for engagement, not like someone explaining something in prose.

This is common in blog and essay humanizing. Do not treat "one idea per sentence" as a goal.

**AI / over-humanized pattern:**

> Portraits were the awkward gap. A studio shot of one or two people is usually a bad fit for this project. I don't know who they are, and colourising their face for a daily post feels wrong. Crowd scenes and tiny background faces are different. I still want those.

**Human alternative:**

> Portraits were the awkward gap for me: a studio shot of one or two people is usually a bad fit, because I don't know who they are and colourising their face for a daily post feels wrong. I still want crowd scenes and tiny background faces in the frame.

**Patterns to watch:**

- Three or more sentences in a row under ~15 words, all declarative
- Each sentence could be its own paragraph or tweet
- A closing one-liner that restates the previous sentence ("X are different. I still want those.")
- No subordinate clauses, no "because" / "which" / "though" linking ideas across sentences

**Fix:** Merge sentences that belong to one thought. Use commas, semicolons, or a single compound sentence with *but*, *because*, or *while*. Keep one short sentence when you want emphasis; do not stack five in a row. Vary length (see `style-rhythm`).

**Not the same as:** `style-punctuation-sparsity` (missing commas in one long sentence) or legitimate bullet-like lists in instructions.
